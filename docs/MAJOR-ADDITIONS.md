# Twenty additions to the catalog

Added 24 October 2026 after reviewing the existing catalog for major gaps. These are twenty defensible additions, rather than a universal ranking of history’s twenty most important events. The selection favors lasting effects, distinct milestones and broader geographic and thematic coverage. Existing biographies and state histories were checked so that these entries add a development rather than repeat a lifespan.

All twenty have authored summaries, claim-specific institutional or academic evidence, separately attributed Wikipedia introductions, reviewed geographic associations, and an editorial priority. Priority **1** means an essential survey milestone; **2** means a major survey topic. The [priority method](PRIORITY.md) explains the full 1–5 scale. All records remain `review`; this pass does not certify every claim in the background articles.

Early Bantu expansion records an uncertain **onset** window, not two thousand years of continuous migration. The Austronesian date marks an approximate departure from Taiwan, not arrival at every later destination. Vedic and Buddhist origins are broad historical orientations, not birthdays for entire religious traditions. Discovery of penicillin is distinct from clinical development and mass production; the transistor’s working demonstration is distinct from its public announcement.

Present-day countries locate associated places without reconstructing historical borders or an exhaustive footprint. Bangladesh in the partition record locates eastern Bengal, then part of Pakistan. The Soviet dissolution record includes all fifteen successor republics; the bare older Georgia label remains untouched and the new record uses an explicit Georgia (country) reference. Site coordinates were not invented or approved.

The [machine-readable review](../data/reports/major-additions-2026-10-24.json) records the full selection rationale, date qualifications, source revisions and place IDs. The requested smallpox-eradication Wikipedia title redirects to the broader History of smallpox article; WHO supplies the evidence for the specific 1980 declaration.
